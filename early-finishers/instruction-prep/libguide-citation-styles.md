# LibGuide: Citation Styles (FICTIONAL SAMPLE)

*Guide owner: Research & Instruction · Audience: undergraduates*

## Why cite?

Citations let readers find your sources, give credit to other people's ideas, and show the
evidence behind your argument. Missing or incorrect citations can count as plagiarism under the
university honor code, even when unintentional.

## Which style should I use?

Ask your instructor first. If they don't specify, these are the common defaults by field:

| Style | Common in | Key feature |
|---|---|---|
| APA 7 | Psychology, education, social sciences | Author–date in text: (Smith, 2021) |
| MLA 9 | English, humanities | Author–page in text: (Smith 42) |
| Chicago | History, some arts | Footnotes plus bibliography |
| ACS / CSE | Chemistry, biology | Numbered references |

## The parts every citation needs

No matter the style, you need to find: **who** wrote it, **when** it was published, **what** it's
called, and **where** it can be found (journal, publisher, URL, or DOI).

## Citation managers

Zotero is free and supported by the library. It saves sources from your browser and formats your
bibliography automatically. Workshops run every month. Check the events calendar.

## A warning about AI tools

AI chatbots often produce citations that look correct but point to articles that don't exist, or
mix up authors, years, and journals. **Never copy a citation from an AI tool without finding the
actual source.** Search the title in the library catalog or Google Scholar, and resolve the DOI at
doi.org.
